## Draft

The boat came in on the grey side of morning, when the water and the sky were the same colour and only the gulls seemed certain which was which. Anna stood at the rail with her one case and watched the salt town gather itself out of the mist: the long sheds, the white scars of the drying pans, the church that leaned a little, as everything here leaned, toward the sea.

She had been away eleven years. The wharf was smaller than the wharf she remembered, and emptier — three boats where there had once been thirty, and half the racks standing bare. Salt did not pay what it had paid. Everyone wrote her that, in the careful letters they sent when her father was dying, as though the failing of the trade might soften the failing of the man.

Above the largest of the sheds, in letters that had been gold and were now the brown of old blood, the name still held: VEREZA & SON. There had never been a son. There had only ever been Anna, and the word, and the long agreement of the town to read it and say nothing.

She lifted her case and went down the gangway into the place that owned her name.
