## Draft

The inn sat low against the road, its windows the colour of weak tea. Anna pushed the door and the talk inside did not stop so much as *bend* toward her, the way grass bends before you feel the wind.

Mara was behind the bar, older, broader, her hands the same red they had always been from the brine. She looked at Anna for a long moment over the rim of a glass she was not really cleaning.

"The daughter," Mara said, to no one, to the room. "Come for what's owed."

"Come to pay what's owed," Anna said. "If there's a difference."

"There's always a difference." Mara set the glass down. "Your father knew the difference better than any man on this coast. It's what he sold, in the end. Not the salt."

The fire spat. Outside the rain had started again, the thin sideways rain that came off the pans and got into everything, and Anna thought she would be tasting salt in her bread for the rest of her life. She put her coin on the bar.

"A room," she said. "And whatever you'd tell a stranger."

Mara almost smiled. "You're no stranger. That's your trouble."
